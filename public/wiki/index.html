01

Your first run

  1. Choose a track. Test Track A is selected by default. All four tracks are available immediately; choosing another reloads the game on that track.
  2. Select Start Run. The car stays at the start through a three-second countdown. The timer begins at GO.
  3. Follow the road to the finish. Checkpoint gates record splits along the way. The goal is a lower elapsed time, rather than points or laps.
  4. Run again. The first completed player run becomes your local best. A faster finish replaces it and its ghost; a slower finish leaves it intact.

Getting comfortable: try short steering inputs and brake before tighter turns. Use R to recover from a mistake, or Enter for a fresh timed attempt.

Gamepad

The game reads the first connected controller exposed by your browser. On a standard mapping, use the left stick to steer, the right trigger for gas and the left trigger for brake. Button 0 (usually A / Cross) resets the checkpoint and confirms from the menu or finish screen. Button 1 (usually B / Circle) restarts; button 9 (usually Start / Options) pauses or resumes. Labels depend on the controller and browser.

04

One car. Automatic gears.

The prototype uses one arcade car with seven automatic forward gears. The speedometer shows km/h and the current gear. There is no car selector, tuning garage or manual gear-change control.

Steering & drift

Steering authority depends on speed, with less authority at high speed and none while stationary. Braking while steering at speed engages a drift state that reduces lateral grip. There is no drift-points system.

Road & barriers

Driving off the road slows the car. Supported road edges have barriers that correct the car's position and reduce or redirect its velocity. An impact can cost speed; there is no damage or health meter.

Jumps & landings

The car can become airborne from ramps or road gaps. Steering in the air is weaker, and hard landings reduce speed. If you miss the road, use a checkpoint reset to recover.

Learning a line

Brake earlier when a bend catches you out, release the brake before you unintentionally reverse, and keep the car near the road when crossing timing gates. These are driving suggestions, not required inputs or bonus conditions.

Read the clock. Recover the car.

Current
Elapsed simulation time for this attempt. Pausing or opening Settings stops its advance.
Best
Your fastest saved player finish on the selected track. Dashes mean there is no saved best.
Split
Total elapsed time at the latest checkpoint crossed. It is not a sector duration or a difference from your best.

Times are displayed as minutes:seconds.milliseconds. Checkpoints register in route order when you cross forward and stay within the gate's road-width tolerance. Crossing the finish forward near the road ends the attempt.

Checkpoint reset versus full restart

  • Press R once: restore the car's recorded pose and velocity from roughly half a second before the most recently passed checkpoint. Before any checkpoint, reset to the start.
  • Press R twice quickly: two presses within 360 milliseconds, after a checkpoint, place the car just beyond that checkpoint with zero velocity.
  • The timer keeps going: a checkpoint reset never rewinds the elapsed time. Driving input is briefly locked for 0.18 seconds after a reset; there is no separately added fixed time penalty.
  • Press Enter for a fresh run: clear the current attempt's time, splits and recording, return to the start, and begin another countdown. Your saved best stays intact.

Rule limit: the reviewed finish check does not require every checkpoint to have been passed. Treat checkpoints as split and recovery gates; the prototype does not certify a complete checkpoint route.

06

Chase a reference, then yourself

Your Best replays the saved fastest player run for this track. It appears after you have a valid saved recording and is controlled by the “Your best ghost” setting.

Codex 5.5 is the built-in model reference. Its ghost and the track's “MODEL PBS” billboard use the same simulated recorded run. The “Model ghosts” setting controls its visibility. Ghosts are visual references; they do not collide with your car.

A finish faster than the model reference reports how much you beat its PB by. Your own best is replaced only by a strictly faster player run. There are no unlocks, currency, medals or online player rankings in this version.

Watch a built-in driving demonstration

Autoplay drives the car automatically and reports its measured finish time. It does not replace your player best. Track C and D use their fastest registered PB driver by default; the other tracks use the default Codex driver.

07

Settings & your local saves

Open Settings from the main menu or pause menu. Changes are saved as you make them.

Audio volume
Adjust from silent to full volume. The default is 65%. Start or resume a run to activate browser audio.
Your best ghost
Show or hide your saved player ghost. On by default; hiding it does not delete the recording.
Model ghosts
Show or hide the built-in reference ghost. On by default; its PB comparison remains available at the finish.
Input overlay
Show steering, gas, brake and input-source readouts during the run. Off by default; I also toggles it.

The game saves settings, the selected track, and one best run with ghost samples per track in your browser's local storage. Returning to the menu, restarting or selecting another track does not intentionally erase those bests.

Keep your saves: use the same browser profile and site address. Records are not synced between devices or browsers. Clearing site data removes them, and private browsing may discard them when the session ends. A local test address has separate storage from the GitHub Pages address.

The wiki itself does not read, write or clear game saves. This version has no save export/import, account or cloud backup. If local storage is blocked or full, saves can fail; the game does not show a dedicated save-error message.

08

What this prototype includes

  • Time attack only. One player car, four available point-to-point tracks and ghost comparisons. No combat, multiplayer race, campaign, unlock tree or vehicle upgrades.
  • Local records only. The model billboard is a built-in reference, not an online player leaderboard.
  • Simulation timing. The clock advances with physics steps rather than directly with wall-clock time. Very slow frames can make an attempt diverge from real elapsed time.
  • Browser-dependent input. Controller mapping and detection depend on your browser. Touch driving is pending verification; no tilt controls are documented.
  • Focused menus are changing. The reviewed baseline uses global game shortcuts. A separate keyboard-menu fix is in progress; this guide does not claim it has shipped.
  • Rendering requirements. The game uses Three.js and WebGL. This text guide can be read without the game renderer or JavaScript.
